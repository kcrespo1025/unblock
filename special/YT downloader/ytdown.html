A small tool for keeping the things you want to keep.

It downloads video and audio from YouTube. It does not show ads, does not watermark, does not keep logs. Paste a link, pick a format, take the file. That is the whole thing.

If you paste a playlist link, it spins up three concurrent workers and fetches them all. No pasting them one by one by one.

— Maintained since November 2019. Single operator. No analytics on this page.

Three steps, no funnel.

01

Paste a link

The tool reads the YouTube page. If it sees list= in the URL, it switches to batch mode and resolves the manifest of every video in the playlist. Nothing is downloaded yet.

02

Pick a format

One format applies to the whole batch. File sizes are computed from the actual stream bitrate and the duration of each video, then summed so you know how big the final archive will be.

03

Take the file (or archive)

For a single video, you get a link. For a playlist, three workers fetch, mux, and zip the files in parallel. The archive link is valid for two hours. No copy is kept.

Questions, briefly.

6 entries
How do the "sub-agents" work for playlists?
They aren't agents in the AI sense — they are just three concurrent worker processes on the server. When you paste a playlist, the tool creates a queue of the videos and assigns them to three workers. As soon as worker-01 finishes a video, it grabs the next one in the queue. This prevents any single download from bottlenecking the whole batch.
Is this legal?
Downloading for personal, offline use is permitted in many jurisdictions; redistributing the file is not. The tool does not bypass DRM, does not access paid content, and refuses age-restricted or members-only videos. If you are unsure whether your use is permitted, do not use it.
Why no 8K option in the dropdown?
Because most videos do not have an 8K stream. The dropdown shows what is generally useful. If a video has a 4320p source, the 2160p option resolves to it; if not, you get the closest available resolution and the size estimate adjusts accordingly.
Where do the files go after I download them?
Nowhere, on the server side. A single file is held for one hour; a batch archive is held for two. After that, they are deleted. The server keeps no record of which URL was requested. Your browser history is your own concern.
Is there an API?
Yes — undocumented and rate-limited to 30 requests per hour per IP. Send a GET to /api/resolve?url=… and you will get JSON back. Do not build a business on this; write to me first.
Why does it look like this?
Because the alternative was a gradient hero, three feature cards with rounded icons, and a "trusted by" strip of grey logos. This is a tool for taking custody of a file. It should look like a tool.
